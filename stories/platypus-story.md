# The River's Riddle

In the quiet streams of eastern Australia, where eucalyptus trees dipped their leaves into the water and the morning mist hung low, there lived a young platypus named Perry. He was an unusual creature—even by platypus standards—with his duck-like bill, beaver tail, and webbed feet that seemed borrowed from an otter.

"What exactly are you?" asked a curious kookaburra one dawn, tilting her head at Perry as he emerged from his burrow.

Perry had heard this question his entire life. With fur like a mammal, a bill like a bird, and venomous spurs on his hind legs like no other creature around, he never quite fit into any category. Even his hunting was peculiar—he closed his eyes underwater and used special sensors in his bill to detect the tiny electrical signals of shrimp and worms hiding in the riverbed.

"I'm a platypus," Perry would say simply. But sometimes, he wondered if being so different was a burden.

One stormy evening, the river swelled dangerously. A family of water rats had been trapped by the rising current in a hollow log, unable to swim against the powerful flow. The other animals gathered on the bank, helpless.

Perry slipped into the churning water. His flat tail steered him expertly through the turbulence. His waterproof fur kept him warm in the frigid depths. When he reached the hollow log, he used his sensitive bill to locate each frightened water rat in the murky darkness, guiding them one by one to safety.

"How did you do that?" gasped the kookaburra, watching the last water rat scramble onto dry ground.

Perry smiled—or at least made the expression a platypus makes when content. "I'm built for exactly this. My tail, my fur, my bill—everything that makes me different also makes me perfect for my home."

The kookaburra laughed her famous laugh, and it echoed through the valley like applause.

From that day forward, when anyone asked Perry what he was, he had a new answer: "I'm exactly what the river needs."
