# The Blobfish Who Found His Confidence

Deep beneath the waves off the coast of Australia, where the water pressure would crush most creatures, lived a blobfish named Bernard. At nearly 4,000 feet below the surface, Bernard's world was dark, cold, and peaceful.

Bernard had a problem, though. Whenever the deep-sea currents carried photographs from the world above, he saw what humans thought of his kind. "World's Ugliest Animal," the captions read. Bernard would sink even lower into the sandy bottom, his gelatinous body drooping with sadness.

"Why so glum?" asked his neighbor, a giant isopod named Iris, her many legs clicking against a rock.

"The surface dwellers mock us," Bernard sighed. "They don't understand."

Iris laughed, a bubbling sound in the deep water. "Bernard, do you know why you look the way you do? Your body is perfectly designed for this pressure. That soft, jelly-like flesh keeps you from being crushed while other fish would be flattened. You don't even need a swim bladder to stay afloat down here."

Bernard had never thought of it that way. His lack of muscle meant he could conserve energy in a world where food was scarce. He could simply hover above the ocean floor, waiting for crabs, sea urchins, and other morsels to drift by. No exhausting swimming required.

"And up there," Iris continued, "where there's no pressure to support your body, of course you'd look different. A human at this depth wouldn't look too pretty either!"

Bernard smiled, which looked like a slight ripple across his pink face. He realized that his body wasn't ugly at all - it was a masterpiece of deep-sea engineering, perfectly adapted to one of Earth's most extreme environments.

From that day forward, Bernard wore his gelatinous form with pride. He was a survivor of the abyss, a creature so specialized that few others could live where he thrived. And whenever the current brought whispers of mockery from above, he simply smiled and thought, "They just don't understand pressure."
