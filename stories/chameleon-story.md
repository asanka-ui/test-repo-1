# The Colors of Courage

In the emerald canopy of a Madagascar rainforest, there lived a young panther chameleon named Kira. Unlike her siblings, who had mastered the art of blending into their surroundings, Kira struggled with her colors. When she tried to turn leaf-green, she became orange. When she aimed for brown bark, she flashed bright turquoise instead.

"You'll never survive," her mother warned, her independently rotating eyes scanning the forest floor for predators. "A chameleon who cannot hide is a chameleon who becomes someone's meal."

Kira practiced every day, gripping branches with her zygodactyl feet—two toes pointing forward, two pointing back—as she concentrated on changing her skin. But her chromatophore cells, those special pigment-containing structures beneath her scales, seemed to have minds of their own.

One morning, a hungry harrier hawk descended toward the forest canopy. Kira's family immediately vanished into the foliage, their skin shifting to perfect camouflage. But Kira panicked. Instead of hiding, her body erupted in a dazzling display of crimson, gold, and electric blue.

The hawk faltered mid-dive, startled by the explosion of color. It had never seen anything like it. In that moment of hesitation, Kira shot out her tongue—a sticky projectile that could extend twice her body length in a fraction of a second—and snatched a passing dragonfly, appearing completely unbothered by the predator.

The hawk, confused and unnerved by this strange, fearless creature, veered away to find easier prey.

From the branches above, Kira's mother watched in amazement. Her daughter hadn't hidden from danger—she had startled it away.

"Perhaps," her mother said, finally rotating both eyes to focus on Kira together, "there is more than one way to survive."

From that day forward, Kira embraced her brilliant colors. She learned that sometimes standing out takes more courage than blending in, and that what makes us different might just be what keeps us alive.
