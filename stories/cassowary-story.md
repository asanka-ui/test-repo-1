# The Guardian of the Rainforest

Deep in the misty rainforests of northern Australia, where ancient trees stretched toward the sky and morning dew glistened on enormous ferns, there lived a southern cassowary named Kira. She stood nearly six feet tall, with glossy black feathers that shimmered blue in the dappled sunlight and a magnificent azure neck that gleamed like sapphires.

Most creatures of the forest gave Kira a wide berth. Her powerful legs ended in feet bearing dagger-like claws, the inner toe sporting a five-inch spike that could slice through almost anything. But Kira had never used her formidable weapons for harm. Instead, she had a far more important purpose.

Every morning, Kira wandered her territory, searching for the fallen fruits of the rainforest. She particularly loved the large, colorful fruits that no other creature could swallow whole. Her favorite were the bright purple Davidson plums and the enormous cassowary plums that shared her name.

What the other animals didn't understand was that Kira was the forest's greatest gardener. Unlike smaller birds who crushed seeds in their beaks, Kira swallowed fruits whole. The seeds passed through her safely and emerged in nutrient-rich packages, ready to sprout into new trees far from their parents.

One dry season, when the forest seemed to hold its breath waiting for rain, old Muru the tree kangaroo approached Kira nervously.

"The young ones are hungry," he said. "The fruit trees have stopped producing."

Kira remembered the secret grove she had planted years ago, far across the river. Seeds she had carried in her belly had grown into a flourishing orchard.

"Follow me," she said, her low, booming call echoing through the trees.

When they arrived, the animals found trees heavy with fruit. Muru finally understood why the elders called cassowaries the keystone of the rainforest.

"You planted all of this?" he asked in wonder.

Kira simply bowed her casque-crowned head. "The forest plants itself through me. I am merely the messenger."

From that day forward, the animals understood that the most fearsome-looking creature in their home was also their greatest protector, carrying the future of the forest within her wherever she roamed.
