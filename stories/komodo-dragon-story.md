# The Patient Hunter of Rinca Island

The morning sun crept over the volcanic hills of Rinca Island, casting long shadows across the dry savanna. Koda, a young Komodo dragon, lay perfectly still beneath a tamarind tree, her forked tongue flicking out to taste the air every few seconds.

At three years old, Koda had finally grown large enough to hunt on the ground. For the first two years of her life, she had lived in the trees, safe from the cannibalistic appetites of larger dragons who wouldn't hesitate to make a meal of their smaller kin. Now, measuring nearly five feet long, she was learning the ancient art of the ambush.

Her tongue detected something promising—the musky scent of a water buffalo calf carried on the breeze. Komodo dragons possess a remarkable sensory organ called the Jacobson's organ, which allows them to "taste" the air and track prey from miles away. Koda processed the information: the calf was upwind, perhaps a quarter mile distant, moving toward the watering hole.

She began her approach, moving with surprising stealth for a creature of her size. Her powerful legs carried her low to the ground, her muscular tail swaying gently for balance. Komodo dragons may appear sluggish, but they can sprint at speeds up to thirteen miles per hour in short bursts.

Koda found her position behind a cluster of palm trees near the water's edge and waited. This was the dragon's greatest weapon—not speed, not even the venomous bacteria-laden saliva that would weaken any prey that escaped an initial strike—but patience. A Komodo dragon could wait for hours, days even, for the perfect moment.

The buffalo calf arrived, flanked by its watchful mother. Koda's yellow eyes tracked their movements, calculating. Today, the mother was too vigilant, the angle wrong. 

Koda settled deeper into her hiding spot, unbothered. She was young, but she had already learned what all great hunters know: there would always be another day, another opportunity. The island had sustained her ancestors for millions of years.

She closed her eyes and waited for tomorrow.
