# The Shrimp Who Shook the Sea

In the warm, shallow waters of a Caribbean coral reef, there lived a small pistol shrimp named Snap. At barely two inches long, she was dwarfed by most of her neighbors, but what she lacked in size, she more than made up for in firepower.

Snap possessed something extraordinary: one oversized claw that could snap shut so fast it created a bubble of superheated water reaching nearly 8,000 degrees Fahrenheit - almost as hot as the surface of the sun. The collapsing bubble produced a thunderous crack louder than a jet engine, enough to stun any unsuspecting prey.

"Show-off," grumbled Gerald the goby fish, who shared a burrow with Snap. This was no accident - pistol shrimp and goby fish often live together in a remarkable partnership. Gerald, with his excellent eyesight, watched for predators while nearly-blind Snap maintained their home. In return, Snap's powerful weapon protected them both.

One afternoon, a shadow fell across their burrow. A hungry moray eel had spotted Gerald hovering near the entrance.

"Snap!" Gerald signaled by touching his companion with his tail fin. "Big trouble!"

The eel lunged, its jaws gaping wide. But Snap was ready. She aimed her massive claw and fired.

CRACK!

The shockwave struck the eel square in the face. Dazed and disoriented, the predator retreated into the reef, reconsidering its lunch plans.

"Not bad for someone who can barely see," Snap clicked cheerfully, already returning to her excavation work.

Gerald settled back into his lookout position. "You know, for a tiny shrimp, you're the loudest creature in this entire ocean."

It was true. Pistol shrimp are among the noisiest animals in the sea, and when many snap together, they create a crackling chorus that can interfere with submarine sonar.

Snap just smiled - or as much as a shrimp can smile - and went back to digging. In the ocean, it pays to speak softly and carry a very big claw.
