# The Ghost of the Mountains

High in the rugged peaks of the Himalayas, where the air grew thin and the winds howled through rocky crevasses, lived a snow leopard named Kira. Her pale gray coat, dappled with dark rosettes, blended so perfectly with the granite and snow that the local villagers called her kind "ghosts of the mountains."

Kira was three years old and had recently claimed her own territory—a vast stretch of alpine terrain spanning nearly forty square miles. Unlike the lions of the African plains or the wolves of the northern forests, snow leopards are solitary creatures who prefer their own company, and Kira was no exception.

One crisp autumn morning, Kira padded silently along a narrow ridge, her massive paws—naturally wide like snowshoes—gripping the icy rocks with ease. Her extraordinarily long tail, almost as long as her entire body, swayed behind her, helping her maintain perfect balance on the treacherous terrain. That magnificent tail also served as a warm blanket on freezing nights, wrapped around her nose and face as she slept.

She paused, her pale green eyes scanning the slopes below. Movement. A herd of blue sheep grazed on a distant cliff face, seemingly secure on their impossible perch. But Kira knew these mountains better than any creature alive. She could leap nearly fifty feet in a single bound—farther than any other cat.

Rather than hunt, Kira continued her patrol. She had eaten well yesterday, and snow leopards, unlike their more aggressive cousins, rarely killed more than they needed. She stopped at a prominent boulder and scraped her scent into the stone, leaving a message for any leopard who might pass through: this territory was claimed.

As the sun began its descent, painting the peaks in shades of gold and pink, Kira settled onto a rocky outcrop that overlooked her entire domain. She was one of fewer than seven thousand snow leopards left in the wild, though she knew nothing of such numbers. She knew only the mountains, the wind, and the ancient rhythms of the high places.

In these moments of stillness, watching the world from her lofty throne, Kira was exactly what the mountains had shaped her to be: patient, powerful, and perfectly at peace.
