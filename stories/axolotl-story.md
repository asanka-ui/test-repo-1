# The Smiling Salamander of Xochimilco

In the ancient canals of Xochimilco, beneath the floating gardens of Mexico City, lived a young axolotl named Xochi. Unlike her cousins who had transformed and left the water long ago, Xochi remained in her juvenile form, with feathery pink gills that swayed like delicate flowers in the current.

"Why don't you grow up and walk on land like the other salamanders?" asked a curious tadpole one morning.

Xochi smiled her famous permanent smile—for axolotls always appear to be grinning—and replied, "Why would I want to change? I have everything I need right here."

And indeed she did. Axolotls possess an extraordinary gift: they can regenerate almost any part of their body. When Xochi once lost a limb to a hungry heron, she simply grew it back, good as new. She could even heal her own heart and brain, a marvel that made the other creatures of the canal regard her with wonder.

One stormy night, the canals flooded with debris and pollution from the city above. Many creatures fled, but Xochi noticed a cluster of fish eggs tangled in plastic, unable to receive fresh water. Using her remarkable regenerative abilities, she worked tirelessly, her sensitive gills filtering out toxins as she cleared the debris piece by piece.

By morning, the eggs were safe, and tiny fish began to hatch. Word spread through the canals of Xochi's bravery.

"You may never walk on land," said an old turtle who had watched everything, "but you have something far more valuable. You know that staying true to yourself and helping others is its own kind of transformation."

Xochi's gills fluttered happily. She dove back into the cool, dark waters of her home, her smile wider than ever, knowing that some of the greatest changes happen not in our bodies, but in our hearts.
