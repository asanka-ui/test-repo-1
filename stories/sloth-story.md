# The Patient Dreamer

High in the misty canopy of the Costa Rican rainforest, a three-toed sloth named Salvador hung upside down from a cecropia branch, watching the world move at its usual frantic pace below.

"Always rushing," Salvador murmured, slowly blinking his gentle eyes. A troop of howler monkeys crashed through the neighboring trees, shrieking about some distant fruit discovery. Salvador simply smiled. He had learned long ago that the sweetest leaves were the ones you took time to savor.

Salvador moved through life at roughly six feet per minute on a good day, and he wouldn't have it any other way. His thick fur, which grew in the opposite direction of most mammals to let rain slide off while he hung inverted, had developed a greenish tint from the algae that made its home there. This living camouflage made him nearly invisible against the verdant canopy, a ghost among the leaves.

One morning, a young capuchin monkey named Mira swung onto his branch, panting heavily. "Sloth! There's a harpy eagle circling! Aren't you frightened? Why don't you run?"

Salvador turned his head with characteristic deliberation, his permanent smile never wavering. "The eagle sees movement, little one. When I am still, I am simply another part of the tree."

Mira watched in amazement as the massive eagle soared past, its keen eyes scanning for prey, passing right over Salvador without a second glance.

"How do you stay so calm?" Mira asked.

"I sleep fifteen hours a day," Salvador replied warmly. "In my dreams, I have already lived a thousand adventures. When I wake, I need not rush to find more. Besides," he added, reaching with agonizing slowness toward a cluster of leaves, "I have learned that everything worth having comes to those who wait."

Mira sat beside him for a while, learning the art of stillness. And for the first time in her young life, she noticed how the afternoon light filtered through the leaves like liquid gold, how the air smelled of orchids and rain, and how beautiful it was simply to be present.

Salvador just smiled his ancient smile. Some lessons, he knew, could only be taught slowly.
