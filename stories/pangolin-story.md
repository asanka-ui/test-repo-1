# The Pangolin's Midnight Garden

In the dense forests of Southeast Asia, where moonlight filtered through the canopy like silver threads, lived a pangolin named Panya. Unlike other creatures who feared the night, Panya treasured it. Her overlapping scales, made of keratin just like human fingernails, gleamed like polished armor as she emerged from her burrow.

Tonight was special. Panya had discovered something extraordinary during her previous foraging expedition—a fallen log teeming with the most delicious termites she had ever tasted. She had marked the path carefully, her poor eyesight compensated by an extraordinary sense of smell that could detect insects buried deep underground.

As Panya waddled through the undergrowth, her long, sticky tongue—nearly as long as her entire body—flickered in anticipation. She moved slowly, deliberately, her powerful claws leaving gentle impressions in the soft earth.

Halfway to her destination, Panya heard a rustling in the bushes. A curious civet emerged, eyeing her with interest. Instinctively, Panya curled into a tight ball, her scales forming an impenetrable fortress. The civet sniffed around the strange sphere, puzzled by this living pinecone, then eventually lost interest and wandered away.

When silence returned, Panya uncurled and continued her journey. At last, she reached the fallen log. Working methodically, she used her powerful forelimbs to tear into the rotting wood, revealing tunnels of termites. Her tongue darted in and out with remarkable speed, collecting hundreds of insects with each sweep.

As dawn approached, Panya's belly was full and content. She ambled back toward her burrow, pausing only to dig a small hole and deposit the seeds she had accidentally collected on her scales—her unintentional gift to the forest that would one day grow into new trees.

The sun's first rays found Panya safe in her underground home, dreaming of termites and starlit walks through her beloved forest.
