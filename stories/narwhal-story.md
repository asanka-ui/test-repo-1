# The Song Beneath the Ice

In the frigid waters of the Arctic Ocean, where icebergs drifted like ancient cathedrals, lived a young narwhal named Nira. Like all narwhals, she bore a long, spiraling tusk that extended from her head like a gleaming sword—though it was actually an overgrown tooth, sensitive enough to detect changes in water temperature and salt content.

Nira belonged to a pod of fifteen narwhals who spent their summers in the shallow coastal waters of the Canadian Arctic. But as autumn approached and the sea ice began to form, it was time for the great migration to deeper waters.

"Stay close," her mother warned. "The ice can trap the unwary."

Narwhals were among the deepest-diving mammals in the world, capable of plunging over a mile beneath the surface. But the journey south required navigating through narrow leads in the ice—channels of open water that could close without warning.

On the third day of migration, Nira heard something extraordinary. A low, haunting sound echoed through the water—clicks and whistles unlike any she had heard before. Curious, she followed the sound away from her pod.

She found an old male narwhal, his tusk worn and chipped from decades of use. He was wedged in a narrowing channel, the ice pressing in around him.

"The songs," he wheezed. "I was following the old songs. The paths my grandmother taught me. But the ice... it moves differently now."

Nira understood. The elders spoke of how the Arctic was changing, the ice less predictable than in generations past.

She pressed her sensitive tusk against the ice wall, feeling for weaknesses, thin spots where the frozen barrier might yield. There—a section where the ice was newer, more fragile.

Using her body weight, she rammed the weak point again and again until cracks spiderwebbed outward. The old male pushed through the gap she created, and together they swam to rejoin the pod.

"You have the gift," the old narwhal told her. "The tusk knows what the eyes cannot see. Remember the old songs, young one, but trust what you feel. The ocean is always teaching those who listen."

As Nira continued the migration with her pod, she began composing her own song—one that would help future generations navigate the changing ice of their Arctic home.
